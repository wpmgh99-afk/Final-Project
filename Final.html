Lin Dan

Lin Dan officially announced his retirement on July 4, 2020, sharing an emotional post on Weibo where he explained that lingering injuries and physical pain at age 37 made it impossible for him to keep fighting alongside the Chinese national team. After hanging up his professional racket, "Super Dan" transitioned into the business world and family life rather than taking on a traditional coaching role. He has focused on successful business ventures, launched his own chain of badminton clubs in Shenzhen called the Lin Dan Badminton Club, and spent quality time supporting his family, including traveling to back his son's athletic training.

Lee Chong Wei

Lee Chong Wei officially announced his retirement on June 13, 2019, with a tearful press conference after doctors advised him that his body could no longer handle the intense physical demands of elite training following his successful recovery from early-stage nose cancer. Putting his health and family first, the Malaysian legend hung up his racket just shy of making a final push for the Tokyo Olympics. After retiring, Lee transitioned into a leadership role within Malaysian sports, serving as the Chef de Mission for the Malaysian contingent at the Tokyo Olympics, dedicating time to his wife and children, and focusing on charity work and youth badminton development to inspire the next generation.

Chen Long

Chen Long officially announced his retirement from the Chinese national badminton team in May 2023, stepping away from international competition after capturing a complete set of Olympic medals—bronze in 2012, gold in 2016, and silver in 2020. After hanging up his professional racket, he chose to prioritize his family, focusing on spending quality time with his wife and young son whom he had spent years away from during grueling training camps and global tournaments. In recognition of his legendary career and impenetrable defensive style, he was subsequently inducted into the Badminton World Federation (BWF) Hall of Fame, cementing his legacy as one of the sport's all-time greats.

Peter Gade

Peter Gade is a Danish badminton legend and one of the most iconic figures in European sports history, renowned for his mesmerizing trick shots, deceptively smooth net play, and graceful, effortless court movement. As a cornerstone of the legendary "Four Kings" era alongside Lin Dan, Lee Chong Wei, and Taufik Hidayat, he spent 62 consecutive weeks as World No. 1 and won five European Championship titles alongside the prestigious All England Open in 1999. Known as the ultimate "Uncrowned King" for remaining at the absolute peak of world badminton for over a decade without an Olympic or World Championship gold, Gade officially retired from professional competition in December 2012 at a farewell tournament appropriately named "Peter Gade's Last Match." After hanging up his racket, he transitioned into coaching, briefly serving as the head coach for the French national badminton team, and has since focused on mentoring young athletes, running his own badminton academy, and spending quality time with his family.

Taufik Hidayat

Taufik Hidayat is an Indonesian badminton legend renowned for possessing one of the most naturally gifted and artistically brilliant playing styles in the history of the sport. A vital pillar of badminton’s famed "Four Kings," he was world-famous for his unmatched backhand—boasting the fastest backhand smash ever recorded—alongside his razor-sharp net play and supreme court vision. At his absolute peak, Taufik captured the Gold Medal at the 2004 Athens Olympics, won the 2005 World Championship, and led Indonesia to multiple Thomas Cup triumphs. He officially retired from professional badminton in June 2013 at the Indonesia Open, concluding a stellar career that redefined flair and creativity in the sport. After stepping away from the court, Taufik transitioned into sports administration, served as the Vice President of Indonesia's Olympic Committee (KOI), and was later appointed as Indonesia's Deputy Minister of Youth and Sports, while also running his own badminton academy to train the next generation of players.

Viktor Axelsen

Viktor Axelsen is a Danish badminton superstar and a dominant titan of the modern era. Standing at nearly 2 meters tall, he leverages his immense height to deliver terrifying steep smashes, paired with an astonishing defensive reach that covers every inch of the court. Axelsen cemented his legacy by winning back-to-back Olympic Gold Medals in men's singles (Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024, alongside a Bronze at Rio 2016), while also claiming multiple World Championship titles and holding the World No. 1 ranking for years. Fluent in Mandarin and deeply respected globally, he stands as the defining figure of post-Four Kings badminton. As an active player still competing at the highest level of international sports, he continues to dominate the circuit and expand his legendary trophy collection.
