Lin Dan is a Chinese sports icon widely celebrated as the greatest badminton player of all time. Nicknamed "Super Dan," the left-handed star dominated men's singles for nearly two decades thanks to his explosive athleticism, lethal jump smashes, and unmatched court coverage. He broke records as the first and only player to complete the "Super Grand Slam"—winning all nine major international titles in badminton—highlighted by two back-to-back Olympic gold medals (2008 and 2012) and five World Championship titles. Beyond his staggering trophy collection, his fiery competitive spirit and legendary rivalry with Malaysia's Lee Chong Wei captivated fans around the world, defining a golden era for the sport until his retirement in 2020.

Lee Chong Wei is a national hero in Malaysia and one of the most revered figures in badminton history, renowned for his unbelievable footwork, defensive mastery, and lightning-fast counter-attacks. Widely known as the "King of Superseries," he dominated the world circuit for years, claiming 69 total titles and holding the record for the most weeks spent as World No. 1 at an astonishing 349 weeks (including 199 consecutive weeks). Though he faced heartbreak on the ultimate stages—earning three consecutive Olympic silver medals (2008, 2012, and 2016) and three World Championship silvers—his incredible consistency, sportsmanship, and heroic battles alongside his great rival Lin Dan won him the respect and admiration of sports fans worldwide.

Chen Long is a Chinese badminton titan who carried the torch of Chinese dominance in men's singles following the era of Lin Dan. Known as the "Great Wall" due to his towering 6-foot-2 frame, incredible reach, and virtually impenetrable defensive style, Chen Long wore down opponents with tireless rallies before launching lethal counter-attacks. Over his stellar career, he achieved the ultimate badminton trifecta by winning the Gold Medal at the 2016 Rio Olympics (alongside a Bronze in 2012 and Silver in 2020), back-to-back World Championships in 2014 and 2015, and holding the World No. 1 ranking. His clinical precision and calm composure under pressure made him one of the most formidable competitors in the history of the sport.

Peter Gade is a Danish badminton legend and one of the most iconic figures in European sports history. As a cornerstone of the legendary "Four Kings" era alongside Lin Dan, Lee Chong Wei, and Taufik Hidayat, Gade was famous for his mesmerizing trick shots, deceptively smooth net play, and graceful, effortless court movement. He spent 62 consecutive weeks as World No. 1 and won five European Championship titles alongside the prestigious All England Open in 1999. Known as the ultimate "Uncrowned King" for remaining at the absolute peak of world badminton for over a decade without an Olympic or World Championship gold, his charisma, sportsmanship, and artistry forever changed how the game was played.

Taufik Hidayat is an Indonesian badminton legend renowned for possessing one of the most naturally gifted and artistically brilliant playing styles in the history of the sport. A vital pillar of badminton’s famed "Four Kings," he was world-famous for his unmatched backhand—boasting the fastest backhand smash ever recorded—alongside his razor-sharp net play and supreme court vision. At his absolute peak, Taufik was virtually unstoppable on the big stage, capturing the Gold Medal at the 2004 Athens Olympics, winning the 2005 World Championship, and leading Indonesia to multiple Thomas Cup triumphs. His charismatic, effortless flair and fiery competitive spirit made him an enduring fan favorite and a true icon of Asian athletics.

Viktor Axelsen is a Danish badminton superstar and a dominant titan of the modern era. Standing at nearly 2 meters tall, he leverages his immense height to deliver terrifying steep smashes, paired with an astonishing defensive reach that covers every inch of the court. Axelsen cemented his legacy by winning back-to-back Olympic Gold Medals in men's singles (Tokyo 2020 and Paris 2024, alongside a Bronze at Rio 2016), while also claiming multiple World Championship titles and holding the World No. 1 ranking for years. Fluent in Mandarin and deeply respected globally, he stands as the defining figure of post-Four Kings badminton.
