Lin Dan

The craft, the comeback, and the legend behind badminton's greatest champion.

Lin Dan, widely known by his iconic nickname “Super Dan,” is universally recognized as the greatest badminton player of all time. Born in Longyan, Fujian, China, in 1983, Lin began playing badminton at just five years old. By the time he retired in 2020, he had redefined the sport through his unmatched athleticism, ferocious competitive spirit, and unparalleled tactical brilliance.

On the court, Lin Dan was a force of nature. A left-handed player, his game was defined by high-octane aggression, lightning-fast footwork, and a signature jump-smash that overwhelmed defenders. Unlike many players who relied solely on speed or technique, Lin possessed an incredible mental fortitude and an innate ability to read his opponents. He could switch seamlessly from a crushing offensive strategy to a deep, patient defensive game, forcing errors from opponents who simply could not break down his rhythm.

Lin Dan's career resume is legendary. By the age of 28, he became the first and only player in history to complete the "Super Grand Slam" by winning all nine major badminton titles: the Olympic Games, World Championships, World Cup, Thomas Cup, Sudirman Cup, BWF World Superseries Finals, All England Open, Asian Games, and Asian Championships. His twin Olympic gold medals in 2008 and 2012 stood out as defining career highlights, particularly his fierce and captivating rivalry with Malaysia’s Lee Chong Wei, which produced some of the most electrifying matches in sports history.

Beyond his incredible stats, Lin Dan transformed the image of badminton on a global scale. Charismatic, bold, and fiercely confident, he brought star power and rock-star flair to the court, breaking away from traditional athlete stereotypes. Inducted into the BWF Hall of Fame in 2023, Super Dan left behind an enduring legacy as the ultimate standard of excellence in badminton.

The 2019 Malaysia Open stands out as one of the most remarkable and emotional chapter triumphs of Lin Dan's late career. Entering the tournament at 35 years old, Super Dan was facing immense scrutiny, having suffered a long title drought and a string of early-round exits over the previous year. Many critics questioned whether the aging legend could still compete at the highest level of world badminton, but Lin Dan arrived in Kuala Lumpur determined to prove that his competitive fire was far from extinguished.

His journey through the tournament was a masterclass in grit, experience, and court presence. Lin Dan battled through a formidable field of younger, faster opponents, including tough victories over top-seeded players like Chou Tien-chen in the opening round and Shi Yuqi in the semifinals. Reaching his first major World Tour final in over a year, he set up a high-stakes, all-Chinese showdown against his longtime national teammate and fellow Olympic champion, Chen Long.

The final match opened as a grueling test of endurance. Chen Long dominated the opening set with razor-sharp defense, quickly taking the lead and putting Lin Dan on the back foot. Undeterred, Super Dan demonstrated the brilliant tactical adaptability that defined his career. In the second and third sets, he adjusted his pace, finding sharper angles and unleashing precise lefty half-smashes that repeatedly broke down Chen Long’s defensive wall. Dictating the tempo of the rallies and controlling the drift on the court, Lin Dan completed a dramatic comeback to secure the championship in three sets.

When the final point landed, an exhausted Lin Dan sank to the floor in relief and triumph, having silenced the doubters and captured his second Malaysia Open title. The victory carried even deeper emotional weight during the podium ceremony, where his greatest rival and close friend, Lee Chong Wei—who was recovering from cancer treatments at the time—presented him with the trophy. It was a triumphant moment that highlighted Lin Dan’s extraordinary durability, mental strength, and undeniable greatness.
